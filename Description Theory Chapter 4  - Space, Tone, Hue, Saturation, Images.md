**Chapter 4: Constructing Spatial Representations from Contrasts**

- **Key Concepts:**
    - Demonstrate how to build a vector-like space from the `(Foreground)>(Background)` principle.
    - Start with one-dimensional sequences of points defined as infinitesimal foregrounds against infinite backgrounds, showing how ordered sets of `(nx) > (n{x-1})` and `(nx) > (n{x+1})` establish direction and measurement.
    - Extend this logic to two dimensions, creating a grid of points each defined by its contrasts with neighbors. Each infinitesimal point is a context: a minimal foreground singled out against an infinitely complex background.
    - Use flow to show how certain paths or directions naturally emerge, giving rise to vector-like frameworks that allow consistent spatial modeling.


**Chapter 5: Describing 2D Images – From Pixels to Patterns of Contrast**

- **Key Concepts:**
    - Apply the spatial logic to 2D images: each pixel (or point) is defined by its contrast to adjacent pixels.
    - Integrate the representation of tone and hue: show that lightness, darkness, and color variations are just more complex `(Foreground)>(Background)` relationships at the pixel level.
    - Demonstrate how an entire 2D image can be encoded as a structured set of contrasts, ultimately showing that any image is, at its core, a field of `(This)>(That)` statements.
    - Address inversion of tones (negative images) to illustrate how the information and flow remain intact despite changes in appearance.


**Chapter 6: Beyond the Canvas – Expanding into 3D and Motion**

- **Key Concepts:**
    - Extend the 2D logic into three dimensions: spaces, volumes, and scenes become layered networks of `(Foreground)>(Background)` relationships.
    - Introduce time and motion: `(Now)>(Then)` as a temporal contrast that lets us describe videos, animations, and dynamic processes.
    - Show how flow and contrast work together to model movement through time, ensuring that the logic which applied to static images also governs sequences of changes (movies, interactive simulations).
